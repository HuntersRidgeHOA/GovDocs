*A plain-English guide to what the Hunter's Ridge Maintenance Association governing documents say, and where each rule is written.*

> **Please read first.** This is a plain-language summary for the board's convenience. It is **not legal advice**. The recorded documents control: if this guide and a document disagree, the document wins. Later state law may change how some provisions apply. **Confirm with the association's attorney before any enforcement action or major decision.**
>
> Prepared October 2026 from the verbatim transcriptions in the `MD/` folder.

- **Dev-era**: written for the developer period; it may no longer apply.

### When documents conflict (order of precedence, as the documents state it)

1. **Cert. of Inc. beats the By-Laws.** (By-Laws Art. XII §2 · p. 10)
2. **The "Documents" beat the By-Laws.** "Documents" means the recorded Plan, both Declarations, their amendments and any later declarations by the developer. (By-Laws Art. XII §2 · p. 10; definition at By-Laws Art. I · p. 1)
3. **By-Laws can't change anything the Cert. or the Documents cover**, except as those documents allow. (By-Laws Art. XII §1 · pp. 9–10)
4. **Board Rules and Regulations can't conflict with the Declaration** (Decl. of Restrictions Art. IV §3 · p. 8 (Bk 819/337)), and no rule takes effect until the Board adopts it (By-Laws Art. IX §3 · p. 8).
5. **Not stated anywhere:** which wins between the Cert. of Inc. and the Declarations, or between the two Declarations. The Easement has no precedence clause either. If two of these collide, ask counsel.

Short version: **the Cert. of Inc. and the Documents outrank the By-Laws, and the Board's Rules must fit within the Declaration.**

## Board playbooks

Steps marked **(good practice)** are recommendations, not requirements of the documents.

### 1. Reviewing an architectural request

1. Confirm the Board holds the review power. The Declaration names the "Declarant," which "may assign" the power to the Board. Find the assignment in HRMA's records; if there is none, ask counsel. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. II §2 · p. 6 (Bk 819/335))
2. Confirm the project needs approval. Check the list: structures, sheds, dishes, fences, walls, pools, solar, screens, exterior changes, color, grade or drainage. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. II §1 · pp. 5–6 (Bk 819/334–335))
3. Date-stamp the submission the day it arrives. **The 30-day clock starts at submission; silence means approval.** (Decl. of Restrictions Art. II §1 · p. 6 (Bk 819/335))
4. Check it is complete: plans and specifications with illustrations showing nature, kind, shape, color, height, materials and location. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. II §1 · p. 6 (Bk 819/335)) Ask promptly for anything missing, in writing (good practice).
5. Check the hard limits in Article I first (e.g., wood-only fences, 120 sq ft sheds, no metal sheds, no above-ground pools, privacy-fence limits). (Decl. of Restrictions Art. I §1–§18 · pp. 2–5 (Bk 819/331–334))
6. Apply the four review factors: harmony, light and air, consistent design and materials, physical impact including drainage. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. II §1 · p. 6 (Bk 819/335))
7. Decide by Board vote (By-Laws Art. VI §5 · p. 5). An approval must be **in writing** (Decl. of Restrictions Art. II §1 · p. 6 (Bk 819/335)). Put rejections in writing too, stating the reasons and citing the rule (good practice; the Declaration requires a written decision only on appeal, Art. II §2 · p. 6 (Bk 819/335)).
8. If the owner appeals in writing: hold a special meeting with the applicant **within 2 weeks** of receiving the appeal, and send the written decision **within 30 days after that meeting**. Missing the 30 days cancels the rejection. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. II §2 · p. 6 (Bk 819/335))
9. File the application, decision and any appeal with HRMA's records (good practice). Members may inspect them. (By-Laws Art. X · p. 9)

### 2. Handling a suspected violation

1. Identify the exact rule and citation in this guide, then read the original (good practice). For Lots 1–5, first check whether Article I applies yet. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. XI §3 · p. 14 (Bk 819/343))
2. Check whether the item was approved, or deemed approved after 30 days. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. II §1 · p. 6 (Bk 819/335))
3. Document it with dates and photos taken from public or common areas (good practice).
4. If a member complained, the responsible committee must receive it and handle or refer it. (By-Laws Art. IX §9 · p. 9)
5. Send a courteous written notice to the owner's address on HRMA's records. Mailed, postage paid, counts as properly sent. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. IX · p. 13 (Bk 819/342)) Give a reasonable time to fix it and offer to talk (good practice; the documents set no cure period or hearing).
6. For misuse of the open space, the Board may suspend privileges only as the Rules and Regulations provide. (By-Laws Art. III §2 · p. 2)
7. If it isn't resolved, consult counsel before any lawsuit (good practice), including on whether HRMA can sue in its own right. The Declaration lets the Declarant or **any owner** sue, and lets the Declarant assign enforcement to HRMA by a recorded document. Remedies are an order to stop, damages or lien enforcement; the loser pays reasonable attorney's fees unless the court orders otherwise. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. VIII · pp. 12–13 (Bk 819/341–342))
8. Delay doesn't waive the rule (Decl. of Restrictions Art. VIII · p. 12 (Bk 819/341)), but treat similar cases the same way (good practice).

### 3. Billing and collecting assessments

1. **Set the amount before March 1.** One rate for every lot. (By-Laws Art. V §2(c)(i) · p. 4; Decl. of Restrictions Art. V §3 · p. 8 (Bk 819/337); 1988 Declaration ¶6(c) · p. 3 (Bk 815/307)) The Cert. says the Board "shall levy" it at the regular Board meeting before the annual meeting. (Cert. of Inc. Seventh · p. 2)
2. **If the amount changes,** get 2/3 of Class A members present at a members' meeting called for that purpose, with written notice stating the purpose. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. V §3 · p. 8 (Bk 819/337)) Notice goes out 10–50 days ahead (By-Laws Art. IV §3 · pp. 2–3) and the 60% quorum applies (By-Laws Art. IV §4 · p. 3). Hold that vote before bills go out (good practice).
3. Update the roster of properties and assessments at the office. (By-Laws Art. V §2(c)(iii) · p. 4)
4. Send every owner written notice of the assessment. (By-Laws Art. V §2(c)(iv) · p. 4) Show the March 1 due date and the 20% discount for payment received before March 31. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. V §4–§5 · p. 9 (Bk 819/338))
5. Deposit payments in Board-approved accounts. (By-Laws Art. VIII §6 · p. 7)
6. **April 1:** anything unpaid is delinquent. Interest at the Delaware legal rate runs from the due date; collection costs and reasonable attorney's fees are added; it's a continuing lien. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. V §6 · p. 9 (Bk 819/338))
7. **Immediately** log each delinquency in the Assessment Lien Docket: entry date, owners' names, lot number, amount, due date, assessment period. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. V §7 · p. 10 (Bk 819/339))
8. Send a mid-March reminder, then a written past-due notice offering a payment plan; if still unpaid, consult counsel (good practice). HRMA may sue the owner personally or foreclose the lien in court. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. V §6 · p. 9 (Bk 819/338); 1988 Declaration ¶6(d) · pp. 3–4 (Bk 815/307–308))
9. If a lender forecloses, HRMA's lien is behind the mortgage. The foreclosure buyer owes only assessments from after the foreclosure. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. V §6 · pp. 9–10 (Bk 819/338–339))
10. When paid, enter the date, the amount and "Paid in Full" in the Docket. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. V §7 · p. 11 (Bk 819/340))
11. **Status-certificate requests** (often at a home sale): on a written request from an owner or a certifying attorney plus $10.00, the Treasurer certifies the lot's status. Include docket details and daily interest if delinquent. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. V §7 · p. 10 (Bk 819/339))
12. **Mail the certificate within 5 business days** after receiving the written request and the $10.00 fee, or every assessment on that lot is deemed paid in full "within the discounted period." Date-stamp requests on arrival (good practice). (Decl. of Restrictions Art. V §7 · pp. 10–11 (Bk 819/339–340))

### 4. Calling and running the annual meeting and elections

1. **Before the meeting:** the Board appoints the Nominating Committee: a director as chair plus one or more members. (By-Laws Art. VII §2 · p. 5)
2. The Committee nominates at least one candidate per open seat; candidates may be non-members. Nominations close before ballots are mailed. (By-Laws Art. VII §3 · p. 5)
3. The Secretary prepares written ballots listing the open seats and nominees, with a write-in space for each seat. (By-Laws Art. VII §4 · pp. 5–6)
4. Mail ballots **at least 14 days before the return date**. Set the return date no later than the day before the meeting. (By-Laws Art. VII §4 · p. 6) The ballot asks for the member's name, signature and number of votes. (By-Laws Art. VII §5 · p. 6)
5. Mail the meeting notice **10 to 50 days ahead**, listing every purpose. (By-Laws Art. IV §3 · pp. 2–3) Include any assessment change (Decl. of Restrictions Art. V §3 · p. 8 (Bk 819/337)) or By-Law amendment (By-Laws Art. XII §1 · pp. 9–10). Mailing ballots and notice together saves postage (good practice).
6. The annual meeting is the **first Tuesday in March at 8:00 p.m.**, at the office or the address in the notice. (By-Laws Art. IV §1 · p. 2)
7. The Secretary keeps returned ballots safe and hands the **unopened** envelopes to a 3-person Election Committee appointed by the Board, on or after the return date and no later than the meeting day. (By-Laws Art. VII §6 · p. 6)
8. At the start, collect written proxies (reject any over 11 months old) and confirm the quorum: **60% of the total membership vote**, in person or by proxy. (By-Laws Art. IV §4 · p. 3)
9. The Election Committee verifies voters, signatures and proxies, counts, and announces results. The most votes wins. (By-Laws Art. VII §6 · p. 6; Art. VII §1 · p. 5)
10. Announce the Nominating Committee members. (By-Laws Art. VII §2 · p. 5)
11. Present the Board's statement of its acts and affairs (By-Laws Art. V §2(a) · p. 3) and the budget and balance sheet (By-Laws Art. VIII §7 · p. 8), after Audit Committee approval (By-Laws Art. IX §7 · p. 9).
12. The Election Committee keeps ballots for 90 days, then destroys them. (By-Laws Art. VII §6 · p. 6)
13. **Right after adjournment,** hold the Board annual meeting: elect officers by majority (By-Laws Art. VI §1 · p. 4; Art. VIII §2 · p. 7) and appoint standing committees (By-Laws Art. IX §1 · p. 8).

### 5. Filling a board vacancy

1. A seat opens by resignation or similar. The Board may also declare a seat vacant after 3 straight missed regular meetings, acting at the meeting of the third absence. (By-Laws Art. V §1(e) · p. 3)
2. The remaining directors fill it by majority vote, even if they are fewer than a quorum. (By-Laws Art. V "Vacancies" · p. 4; Cert. of Inc. Seventh · p. 2)
3. Note the term. The Cert. says the appointee serves until the next annual members' meeting; the By-Laws say the rest of the predecessor's term; the Cert. controls over the By-Laws. (Cert. of Inc. Seventh · p. 2; By-Laws Art. V "Vacancies" · p. 4; Art. XII §2 · p. 10) Put the seat on the next annual ballot unless counsel advises otherwise (good practice).
4. Keep at least 3 directors (By-Laws Art. V §1 · p. 3). The President and at least one VP must be directors. (By-Laws Art. VIII §1 · pp. 6–7) If the departing director held office, the Board may appoint a replacement. (By-Laws Art. V §1(a) · p. 3)
5. If the vote happens other than at a regularly called meeting, have **every** director assent in writing to the record of the meeting; the majority's action is then valid. Whether this covers action with no meeting at all is unclear. (By-Laws Art. VI §4 · p. 5)
6. Record the action in the minutes. (By-Laws Art. VIII §5 · p. 7) Tell the members (good practice).

### 6. Amending the Declaration or By-Laws

1. Have counsel draft every amendment (good practice).
2. **Decl. of Restrictions:** collect signatures of record owners of **2/3 of the lots** on a written document, then record it with the New Castle County Recorder of Deeds. (Decl. of Restrictions Art. VI · p. 11 (Bk 819/340)) If there are 72 lots (Exhibit A lists Lots 1–72), that would be 48; confirm the current lot count from the recorded Plan (good practice).
3. **1988 Declaration:** no change is valid without **New Castle County Council approval**. (1988 Declaration ¶16 · pp. 6–7 (Bk 815/310–311)) Talk to the County early (good practice).
4. **By-Laws:** the documents conflict on who amends. The By-Laws say members, by a majority of a quorum at a meeting whose notice states the purpose. (By-Laws Art. XII §1 · pp. 9–10; Art. IV §3 · pp. 2–3) The Cert. says the Directors. (Cert. of Inc. Sixth · p. 2) The Cert. controls over the By-Laws. (By-Laws Art. XII §2 · p. 10) Until counsel advises, consider having both the Board and the members approve (good practice).
5. A By-Law change can't alter anything the Cert. or the Documents cover, except as they allow. (By-Laws Art. XII §1 · pp. 9–10)
6. **Rules and Regulations** (not an amendment of the documents): the Rules Committee drafts at the Board's request; the Board adopts and publishes. Rules can't conflict with the Declaration. (By-Laws Art. IX §3 · p. 8; Art. V §1(c) · p. 3; Decl. of Restrictions Art. IV §3 · p. 8 (Bk 819/337))
7. Once adopted, file copies with HRMA's records and send them to all members (good practice).

### Conflicts and inconsistencies between documents

1. **Who amends the By-Laws.** Cert.: the Directors (Cert. of Inc. Sixth · p. 2). By-Laws: the members (By-Laws Art. XII §1 · pp. 9–10). The Cert. controls over the By-Laws (By-Laws Art. XII §2 · p. 10).
2. **How long a vacancy appointee serves.** Cert.: until the next annual members' meeting (Cert. of Inc. Seventh · p. 2). By-Laws: the predecessor's unexpired term (By-Laws Art. V "Vacancies" · p. 4).
3. **Who sets the annual assessment.** The Board fixes it (1988 Declaration ¶6(c) · p. 3 (Bk 815/307); By-Laws Art. V §2(c)(i) · p. 4; Cert. of Inc. Seventh · p. 2). But "any change" needs 2/3 of Class A members present (Decl. of Restrictions Art. V §3 · p. 8 (Bk 819/337)). The 1988 Declaration also leaves staffing and insurance spending to "members ... or the majority of them" (1988 Declaration ¶6(b) · p. 3 (Bk 815/307)).
4. **When the assessment is set.** Cert.: at the regular Board meeting before the annual meeting (Cert. of Inc. Seventh · p. 2). Under the By-Laws schedule, unless the Board has changed it, that is the December meeting (By-Laws Art. VI §2 · p. 4). By-Laws: as soon as practical after the calendar year begins, before March 1 (By-Laws Art. V §2(c)(i) · p. 4).
5. **When an assessment is late.** 1988: delinquent when not paid when due; if still unpaid 30 days after the due date, interest runs from the date of delinquency (1988 Declaration ¶6(d) · pp. 3–4 (Bk 815/307–308)). Decl. of Restrictions: delinquent if unpaid before April 1, interest from the due date (Decl. of Restrictions Art. V §6 · p. 9 (Bk 819/338)). The two land near the same date but are worded differently.
6. **Status certificates.** Decl. of Restrictions: written request from an owner or certifying attorney, $10.00, 5 business days (Decl. of Restrictions Art. V §7 · pp. 10–11 (Bk 819/339–340)). By-Laws: any person, on demand, no fee stated (By-Laws Art. V §2(d) · p. 4). The Documents control over the By-Laws (By-Laws Art. XII §2 · p. 10).
7. **Who approves the budget.** The Treasurer prepares it (By-Laws Art. VIII §7 · p. 8), the Audit Committee "approves" it (By-Laws Art. IX §7 · p. 9), and spending is tied to a budget "adopted by the Board" (By-Laws Art. VIII §6 · p. 7). Safest to do all three (good practice).
8. **Officer list.** The Cert. lets the Board elect a President, a Vice-President, a Secretary, a Treasurer "and such other officers as may be deemed desirable" (Cert. of Inc. Seventh · p. 2), so Assistant Secretaries are allowed under the Cert. The By-Laws also require one or more Assistant Secretaries (By-Laws Art. VIII §1 · pp. 6–7) and mention an "Executive Vice-President" that no article creates (By-Laws Art. IV §2 · p. 2). The 1990 Written Consent named no Assistant Secretary and combined Secretary and Treasurer (By-Laws Written Consent · p. 11). The documents don't say whether one person may hold two offices.
9. **Who "Declarant" is in the 1988 Declaration.** The opening paragraph names the Schlossers and Granville (1988 Declaration Preamble · p. 1 (Bk 815/305)); the definition names only Granville (1988 Declaration ¶14(e) · p. 6 (Bk 815/310)).
10. **Open-space buildings vs. trees.** Buildings are allowed (1988 Declaration ¶3 · p. 2 (Bk 815/306)) but existing trees and plants must stay undisturbed (1988 Declaration ¶5 · p. 2 (Bk 815/306)).
11. **Names that don't match.** The Plan is a "Land Subdivision Plan" in one By-Laws definition and a "Land Development Plan" in the next (By-Laws Art. I · p. 1). The Easement's plan title says "Hunter's Ridge Association" (Easement ¶1 · p. 1) and its notary block says "Hunter's Ridge Maintenance Corporation" (Easement · p. 4 (Bk 2840/311[?])).

### Ambiguous wording

- **"Majority of a quorum"** for By-Law amendments: a majority of votes present, or a majority of the minimum quorum (more than 30% of the total vote)? (By-Laws Art. XII §1 · pp. 9–10)

### What the documents don't address

- **Flags.**
- **Director term length, maximum board size,** and how the number of directors is set. (Cert. of Inc. Seventh · p. 2 leaves it to the By-Laws; By-Laws Art. V §1 · p. 3 says only "three (3) or more.")
- **Members removing a director.**
- **What happens if a members' meeting misses the 60% quorum.** There is no adjournment or reduced-quorum rule.
